EXPANSION IDENTITIES FOR THE FIVE VEBLEN ROWS — scout, 2026-08-10
=================================================================

Measurement record for the `Evidence/Cert.lean` side of `SqvDecomp`.  Two of the
five identities existed, two were written (commit f355bad), one is scouted and
named but NOT written.  Everything below was measured, not inferred.

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
--------------------
`SqvDecomp t fs' := ∀ n, BMS.expand (sqv' t) n = sqv' (fs' n)` needs, per row, a
`Cert`-side identity saying what `expand?` of that row's matrix IS.  The encoder
facts (`Evidence/SqV.lean` §17–§21) are the other half and were all proved first.

The binding constraint on the remaining rows turned out to be these identities,
NOT the shape of the assembly — which was found by one grep of `Cert.lean`'s
`expand_` theorems after five encoder facts had been proved on the assumption
that the consumer side was uniform across the rows it serves.  It is not.

FOUR OF THE FIVE ARE ONE SHAPE
------------------------------
    expand? M n = some (take k M ++ (replicate (n+1) B).flatten)

    row                k   B
    ω^(ε₀+1)           0   [[0,0],[1,1]]
    ε_ω                1   [[1,1]]
    ε_{ω²}             1   [[1,1],[2,0]]
    ε_{ω^ω}            2   [[2,0]]

The `n` vs `n+1` discrepancy is absorbed by the take index rather than being a
second parameter.

WHAT IS SHARED AND WHAT IS NOT — the reason no general lemma was written
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Shared, and parameterisable:
    hblk : ∀ a, (…) = <constant block>      by `intro a; rfl`
    then  rw [map_congr_left …, map_const_flatten]; rfl

NOT parameterisable: the `show`.  It asserts how `expand?` REDUCES for a specific
`M`, and the root index and block width are computed from `M` by the definition.
A general lemma would have to take that reduction as a hypothesis — which is the
whole content.  It would save three lines of about twenty.

COORDINATOR DECISION (2026-08-10): write the four specific identities.  A lemma
whose hypothesis is its content does not earn a name, and the per-row `show` is
`rfl`-dischargeable, so the cost of NOT generalising is three lines per row.

Four callers made the shared part and the unshared part visible at the same time.
That is the honest outcome of "write the specific lemma first and generalise when
a second caller appears": here callers two through four said do not.

ε_{ε₀} IS A DIFFERENT SHAPE, AND BOTH SIDES AGREE THAT IT IS
-------------------------------------------------------------
    expand [[0,0],[1,1],[2,0],[3,1]] n
      == [[0,0],[1,1]] ++ (range (n+1)).map (fun a => [a+2, 0])     true, n < 5

The block GROWS with the index instead of repeating, so no constant-block
statement can express it: the block's CONSTANCY is what varies, and a lemma over
a constant `B` cannot express a `B` indexed by position.

`Cert.lean` already has  towerM n = (range (n+1)).map (fun a => [a, 0])  and the
tail above is `towerM` SHIFTED BY 2 exactly.  So state the identity over a
base-parameterised `towerM`, `(range (n+1)).map (fun a => [a + b, 0])` with
`b = 0` recovering `towerM`, rather than defining a second name for one shape.
(Ninth time in this session that the ancestor already existed.)

THE TWO-SIDED AGREEMENT, AND ITS OPPOSITE
------------------------------------------
`Evidence/SqV.lean` §21 found ε_{ε₀} to be the first row needing an induction on
`n`, because the BLOCK's own encoding grows.  The matrix side independently says
the same row is the different one, and the two expressions coincide:

    matrix side   (range (n+1)).map (fun a => [a + b, 0])
    term side     ladderCols d (n+1) = (range (n+1)).map (fun i => (i + d, 0))

Same expression up to `Col2` versus `List Nat`.  THE CORRESPONDENCE TRANSFERS
THIS SHAPE EXACTLY.

Contrast `Evidence/SqV.lean` §23, where it transfers not at all: `oR` of the ε_n
rungs' expansions is `ω^(ε₀·2)`, `ω^(ω^(ε₀·2))`, … — genuinely a tower — while
the matrices are a two-column-per-step family `[j,0],[j+1,1]`, which is neither
`towerM` (one column per step) nor `famM` (alternating).  "IT IS A TOWER" IS TRUE
OF THE ORDINALS AND FALSE OF THE MATRICES.

Both cases are recorded together deliberately.  This repo exists to establish the
correspondence between the two sides; a reader with only one example will
generalise from it, and either generalisation is wrong.

THE CHECK, AND WHERE IT HAD TO LIVE
------------------------------------
All three new identities were `#guard`ed against `sqv'` at n < 5 BEFORE being
proved:

    expand [[0,0],[1,1],[2,0],[2,0]] n == sqv' (fsEW2 n)   true ×5
    expand [[0,0],[1,1],[2,0],[3,0]] n == sqv' (fsEWW n)   true ×5
    expand [[0,0],[1,1],[2,0],[3,1]] n == sqv' (fsEE  n)   true ×5

No finding — all three are the right statements.  A check that passes is still
evidence, and here it is the evidence that three `rfl`-shaped theorems are about
the matrices we think they are: a wrong matrix would have built green.

The guards live in `Evidence/SqV.lean`, not beside the theorems, because `Cert`
must never cite `sqv'` — the import arrow is `SqV → Cert` and it is enforced by
the module graph rather than by anyone remembering.  §20.1 records the split so a
reader does not conclude the check was skipped.
